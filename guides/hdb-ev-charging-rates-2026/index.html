The Complete Guide to HDB EV Charging Rates (2026)

By Where2Charge Team · 23 October 2026 · 12 min read

If you drive an electric vehicle (EV) in Singapore, charging at an HDB multi-storey carpark is no longer just a backup option. For drivers without home charging, public chargers in residential estates can be an important part of everyday life.

But knowing where to charge is only half the problem. You also need to know how much you will pay, whether the charging bay is available, and what happens if you leave your vehicle plugged in after charging finishes.

With SP Mobility's acquisition of ChargEco, new competition safeguards for East Singapore, and idle fees becoming an increasingly important consideration, choosing a charger requires more than comparing prices.

This guide explains HDB EV charging rates in 2026, the difference between AC and DC charging, the new rules drivers should know about, and how to find an available charging lot before leaving home.

TL;DR: HDB EV Charging Rates in 2026

  • Typical charging cost: Singapore's Ministry of Transport reported a median public charging price of S$0.66/kWh at HDB carparks in September 2025. Actual prices vary by operator, location and charger power.
  • AC charging: Generally suited to longer parking periods and overnight charging.
  • DC fast charging: Better suited to quicker top-ups, where available.
  • East Singapore price protection: SP Mobility's commitments following its ChargEco acquisition restrict price increases at specified East HDB charging points until 27 May 2029, subject to limited exceptions.
  • Idle fees: SP Mobility charges S$0.50 per minute after the applicable 30-minute grace period, capped at S$40 per session. Its current published schedule applies between 10 AM and 10 PM at covered non-petrol-station locations.
  • The smarter way to charge: Check live charger availability and compare locations on Where2Charge before driving to a charging bay.

The Ministry of Transport's published median is a useful benchmark, not a guarantee of today's rate at every HDB charger. Source: Ministry of Transport

1. AC vs DC: Which HDB Charger Should You Choose?

Not all public EV chargers deliver power at the same speed. Understanding the difference between AC and DC charging helps you decide whether to prioritise a lower price or a shorter charging stop.

AC charging: The everyday option

AC chargers are common in residential carparks. They supply alternating current, which your vehicle converts into the direct current required by its battery.

Depending on the vehicle and charger, residential charging points may offer power ratings such as 7.4 kW or 22 kW. Your actual charging speed depends on the vehicle's onboard AC charging capability, the charger's output and other conditions.

Best for:

  • Residents parking for several hours.
  • Drivers charging while visiting friends or running errands.
  • Longer parking periods when charging speed is less important.

AC charging is often a practical choice when your car will be parked for a while anyway.

DC fast charging: When time matters

DC chargers supply direct current to the vehicle's battery, bypassing the vehicle's onboard AC charger. Depending on the charger and your vehicle, power ratings may range from around 50 kW to 150 kW or more.

Charging from 20% to 80% can sometimes take approximately 30–45 minutes on a suitable fast charger, but this is not guaranteed. Battery size, temperature, vehicle charging limits and the battery's current state of charge all affect charging speed.

Best for:

  • Drivers who need a quick top-up.
  • Private-hire drivers trying to minimise downtime.
  • Drivers preparing for a longer journey.
  • EV owners whose usual charging location is unavailable.

What will it cost?

Charging prices are normally calculated using the energy consumed, measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh).

Remember: A faster charger does not automatically cost more at every location. Check the applicable rate for the particular charging point before starting your session.

2. SP Mobility, Charge+ and CDG ENERGY: Comparing HDB Charging Networks

Singapore has multiple public charging operators. Their networks, charging speeds, pricing and app features differ, so it is worth comparing individual locations rather than assuming one operator is always cheapest.

SP Mobility: A major public charging network

SP Mobility operates public charging points across residential and commercial locations. It also operates Singapore's first EV fast-charging hub within an HDB carpark, launched in Tengah in May 2026.

When comparing an SP Mobility charger, check:

  • The displayed price per kWh.
  • The charging point's power rating.
  • Whether idle fees apply at that location.
  • The applicable idle-fee hours and grace period.

Official information: SP Mobility

Charge+: An alternative worth comparing

Charge+ operates public charging points across Singapore, including residential locations.

If you find a Charge+ charger at the next HDB block, compare its displayed rate against nearby alternatives. A lower price may be attractive, but only if the charger is suitable for your vehicle and available when you arrive.

Official information: Charge+

CDG ENERGY: Check the individual charging point

CDG ENERGY, formerly known as CDG ENGIE, is another operator to consider when looking for public EV charging in Singapore.

Check the current price, charger power and any applicable session or idle fees before charging. Do not assume that rates or rules are identical across every network.

3. What the SP Mobility–ChargEco Acquisition Means for East Singapore

One of the most significant developments in Singapore's public EV charging market in 2026 was SP Mobility's acquisition of ChargEco, the joint venture previously operated by Strides and YTL PowerSeraya.

The Competition and Consumer Commission of Singapore (CCS) conditionally approved the proposed acquisition on 28 May 2026. The subsequent transfer of more than 1,000 ChargEco charging points to SP Mobility began on 30 June 2026.

The development matters particularly to drivers in the East, where both operators had previously competed for HDB charging demand.

Does this mean East Singapore charging prices will rise?

Not automatically.

To address competition concerns, SP Mobility committed that prices at the specified East HDB charging points covered by the agreement would not exceed pre-transaction levels, except in certain permitted circumstances.

The commitments run from 28 May 2026 to 27 May 2029. They also include protections against discriminatory application of discounts and rebates based solely on charging in the East.

This is more precise than saying that every charging price across the East or Central regions is frozen. The commitment applies to the specified HDB charging points covered by the agreement, not every charger operated by SP Mobility or ChargEco nationwide.

What drivers should do: Continue comparing actual tariffs at individual locations. A price-protection commitment does not necessarily mean that all chargers have the same price or that the cheapest charger will always be available.

4. Warning: Understand EV Charging Idle Fees

A completed charging session does not mean you can leave your vehicle connected indefinitely.

Idle fees are designed to discourage drivers from occupying charging bays after their charging session has ended, allowing other EV drivers to use the equipment.

These fees are separate from the electricity consumed during charging. Depending on the operator and location, they may add a significant amount to your total cost.

SP Mobility's idle-fee rules

SP Mobility published an update to its idle-fee schedule in August 2026, effective from 5 September 2026.

For covered locations other than petrol stations, the published rules are:

Rule SP Mobility
Idle fee S$0.50 per minute
Grace period 30 minutes after charging completes
Maximum fee S$40 per session
Applicable hours 10 AM–10 PM daily
Petrol stations Idle fees apply throughout the day

The applicable conditions should be checked in the SP Mobility app for your specific location.

The 30-minute grace period starts when the charging session completes, not when you first plug in your vehicle. The charging session may end before the battery reaches 100%, so pay attention to session-completion notifications.

At S$0.50 per minute, 60 chargeable idle minutes would cost S$30. The S$40 cap is equivalent to 80 chargeable minutes after the grace period.

Official source: SP Mobility — Update on Idle Fee

Do other operators have the same rules?

Do not assume so. Idle-fee rates, grace periods, operating hours and maximum charges can differ by operator and location.

Before starting a session:

  1. Open the operator's app and review the charging point's conditions.
  2. Enable charging-completion notifications.
  3. Allow enough time to return to your vehicle.
  4. Move your vehicle promptly after charging ends.

Practical tip: If you are charging while having lunch or shopping, choose a location where you can comfortably return to your car before any applicable grace period expires.

5. The “Secret” HDB Fast Chargers: Look Beyond Standard AC Lots

Many drivers associate HDB charging with slower AC chargers. However, Singapore is also introducing higher-powered DC charging facilities within residential estates.

One notable example is the first HDB carpark fast-charging hub at Tengah.

Tengah's 100 kW fast-charging hub

Launched on 16 May 2026, the hub serves HDB Blocks 123 and 129 beside Plantation Plaza.

It comprises:

  • Eight DC fast-charging points rated at up to 100 kW each.
  • Six AC charging points rated at 7.4 kW each.

That gives drivers a choice between faster charging and longer-duration charging at the same general location.

The facility is particularly useful for drivers who need a quicker top-up while running errands or having a meal.

Source: SP Group — Singapore's first HDB fast-charging hub

How to find more fast chargers

Do not limit your search to the carpark closest to your home. A nearby estate may offer a faster charger that better suits your schedule.

When searching, filter for:

  • DC charging.
  • Your vehicle's compatible connector.
  • The charging power you need.
  • Availability at the time you plan to arrive.
  • The displayed charging rate.

Remember that a 100 kW charger does not guarantee that your vehicle will charge at 100 kW throughout the session. Your vehicle's maximum DC charging capability and battery conditions still matter.
