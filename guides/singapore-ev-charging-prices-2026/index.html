Singapore's EV Charging Price Gap: How to Save Money on Every Charge

By Where2Charge Team · 26 October 2026 · 7 min read

Why Do EV Charging Prices Vary So Much in Singapore?

Electric vehicle (EV) charging prices in Singapore can vary significantly between charging operators, locations and charging speeds. Two charging points that appear close together on a map may charge different rates per kilowatt-hour (kWh), even when both can charge your vehicle.

Based on the Where2Charge pricing dataset reviewed on 26 October 2026, the median listed price was approximately S$0.695/kWh for AC charging and S$0.80/kWh for DC charging. The lowest positive rates in the dataset were approximately S$0.3379/kWh for AC charging and S$0.39/kWh for DC charging.

These figures are a snapshot of the dataset, not official tariffs set by the Land Transport Authority (LTA). Charging operators set their own prices, and prices and access conditions can change.

For drivers who charge regularly, understanding these differences can make a meaningful difference to monthly running costs.

A Charger Just 100 Metres Away Could Save You Money

Many EV drivers naturally choose the nearest charging point. That is convenient, but it may not always be the cheapest practical option.

Imagine two charging stations near your destination. (Charging at an HDB carpark? See our guide to HDB EV charging rates in 2026.) One charges S$0.69/kWh, while another charges S$0.50/kWh. If both are accessible to you, compatible with your vehicle and available when you arrive, walking an extra 100 metres could be worthwhile.

For a 40 kWh charging session:

Price per kWh Cost for 40 kWh
S$0.50 S$20.00
S$0.69 S$27.60
Potential saving S$7.60

If you make four similar charging stops a month, that difference could add up to S$30.40 per month, or S$364.80 over a year.

These are illustrative calculations, not a guarantee of savings. The actual amount depends on the energy you charge, the rates available to you and any additional parking or service fees.

The takeaway: Do not compare prices alone. Compare the cheapest charger you can actually use, the distance to reach it, its availability and the total cost of parking and charging.

The Cheapest Charger May Not Be Open to the Public

One important detail can make headline charging prices misleading: not every charging point listed in a dataset is available for public use.

For example, Schneider Electric Building at 50 Kallang Avenue appeared in the reviewed dataset with an AC charging rate of approximately S$0.3379/kWh. However, this is a private, restricted-access location, not a charger that every member of the public can simply drive up to and use.

That distinction matters. A low price at a private office building, industrial property or other restricted site may be useful to authorised users, but it should not be treated as a realistic saving for every EV driver.

When comparing Singapore EV charging prices, it helps to distinguish between three types of locations:

  • Publicly accessible chargers: Available for general use, subject to site opening hours, parking rules and other conditions.
  • Private-site chargers with visitor access: Located on private premises but potentially available to authorised visitors, customers or guests.
  • Restricted-access chargers: Reserved for employees, tenants, residents, members or other authorised users.

Private chargers may have lower rates because of the site's commercial arrangements, intended users or management policies. However, private ownership does not automatically mean lower prices, and public accessibility does not automatically mean higher prices.

Before travelling to a cheaper charger, check whether you can enter the site, whether charging is available to your user category, and whether parking or entry charges apply.

A useful price comparison should help drivers find the cheapest accessible option, not simply identify the lowest number in a database.

AC vs DC Charging: Which Is Cheaper?

The type of charging also affects the price.

AC charging

AC chargers are commonly found in residential car parks, offices and destinations where drivers leave their vehicles for longer periods. They are often suitable for overnight charging, workdays or errands.

In the reviewed Where2Charge dataset, the median listed AC rate was approximately S$0.695/kWh.

DC charging

DC fast chargers can replenish a vehicle's battery more quickly, depending on the vehicle's charging capability and battery conditions. They can be useful when time is limited or when a driver needs to continue a journey.

The median listed DC rate in the dataset was approximately S$0.80/kWh.

These are dataset medians, not universal rules. Some AC chargers may cost more than some DC chargers. Compare the actual price, charging speed, accessibility and parking cost for the location you intend to use.

How Much Can You Save by Choosing a Cheaper Charger?

Consider a 40 kWh charging session at three illustrative price points:

Charging rate Cost for 40 kWh
S$0.34/kWh S$13.60
S$0.69/kWh S$27.60
S$0.89/kWh S$35.60

At these rates, choosing S$0.34/kWh instead of S$0.69/kWh would save S$14 for that session. Choosing S$0.34/kWh instead of S$0.89/kWh would save S$22.

If a driver could consistently access the cheaper option for four comparable sessions each month, the difference versus S$0.89/kWh would be S$88 per month, or S$1,056 per year.

But there is an important qualification: these comparisons are only meaningful when the chargers are genuinely usable by that driver. A restricted private charger is not a practical alternative if access is unavailable. Parking fees, detours, charging speed and the energy actually required can also change the result.

Do Not Forget Parking Fees

The price per kWh is only part of the cost of charging an EV in Singapore.

A charger with a low energy rate may be located in a car park with substantial parking charges. Another charger may have a slightly higher charging rate but offer free parking or a more convenient location.

For example, if one charging session saves S$3 in electricity but incurs S$5 more in parking fees, the cheaper energy rate may not produce the lowest total cost.

When comparing chargers, check:

  • Charging price per kWh
  • Parking fees and any free-parking period
  • Whether the site is open and accessible to you
  • Charger availability and the freshness of the status information
  • AC or DC charging speed and connector compatibility
  • Any idle fees or other operator charges

Idle fees are easy to overlook: read how they work at HDB chargers. Do not assume that a missing price means charging is free. Likewise, check whether a displayed price is current and whether it applies to your chosen charging point.

How to Find a Cheaper Available Charger Near You

This is where a charging map can make a difference. Open the Where2Charge live map and follow along. Instead of choosing a charger based only on familiarity or distance, compare nearby options before you set off.

With Where2Charge, EV drivers can use the map to explore charging locations across Singapore, check available charger information and compare listed prices where reliable pricing data is available.

A simple routine can help you avoid unnecessary costs:

  1. Search for chargers near your destination.
  2. Compare the listed price per kWh, charging speed and connector type.
  3. Check availability and whether the location is accessible to the public or to you personally.
  4. Check parking charges separately from charging costs.
  5. Consider nearby alternatives. A charger just 100 metres farther away may offer a better overall deal.
  6. Recheck the details before travelling, because prices, access rules and availability can change.

The goal is not to drive across Singapore for the cheapest rate. It is to find a cheaper, suitable charger that is close enough and practical for your journey.

The Bottom Line: Compare the Price You Can Actually Pay

Singapore's EV charging market offers a range of prices, but the lowest listed price does not always represent the best option for every driver. Some low-priced charging points are located on private premises, and access restrictions can make them unsuitable for the general public.

For everyday savings, compare chargers that you can genuinely use. Look at the charging rate, parking fees, availability, speed and distance together.

Sometimes, the best saving is not a long detour or a complicated charging plan. It may simply be choosing a compatible, accessible charger 100 metres away instead of the one you normally use.
