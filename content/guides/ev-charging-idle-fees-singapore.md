As Singapore pushes toward its goal of comprehensive EV infrastructure, charging networks are fighting lot congestion with a new weapon: the idle fee. For new EV owners, learning how to charge is only half the battle; learning when to unplug is now just as critical for your wallet.

While we previously highlighted the dangers of uncapped networks like Tesla, there is a hidden trap even within the "capped" networks. For many drivers, hitting the maximum idle fee cap is mathematically equivalent to paying for an entire second battery charge.

Here is exactly how the math works and why you cannot afford to ignore your charging app notifications.

## What Is an Idle Fee?

Idle fees are additional penalties that apply when an EV remains plugged into a charger after the charging session has ended and the grace period (typically 30 minutes) has expired. For a closer look at one operator's schedule, see our [guide to HDB EV charging rates and idle fees](/guides/hdb-ev-charging-rates-2026/#4-warning-understand-ev-charging-idle-fees).

## The "Capped" Providers in Singapore

To prevent runaway bills, several major operators have instituted maximum limits per session:

- **SP Mobility:** Charges $0.50 per minute, capped at $40 per session.
- **Shell Recharge:** Charges $0.50 per minute, capped at $40 per session.
- **ChargEco:** Charges $0.50 per minute, capped at $20 per session.
- *(Note: Tesla sets no upper limit, meaning fees can accrue indefinitely.)*

## The Reality Check: The Cost of a Penalty vs. The Cost of Power

To understand the true weight of a $40 penalty, you have to look at the current average price of electricity. As of late 2026, the average cost for standard Type 2 (AC) charging across Singapore is roughly $0.69 per kWh.

Let's look at a real-world example using a standard commuter EV, like a BYD Dolphin or a standard-range MG4, which typically have battery capacities around 45 to 50 kWh.

- **The Cost to Charge:** If you plug in at 20% and charge up to 100%, you are pulling about 40 kWh of energy. At the average AC rate of $0.69/kWh, your actual charging session costs $27.60.
- **The Cost to Overstay:** If you leave your car plugged in at an SP Mobility or Shell Recharge lot and ignore the app notifications, your 30-minute grace period expires. After 80 minutes of overstaying at $0.50 per minute, you hit the maximum cap of $40.00.

## The Verdict

In this extremely common scenario, your penalty fee ($40) is significantly more expensive than the electricity you actually used to power your car ($27.60).

Essentially, overstaying your welcome at a capped charger means you are paying for nearly one and a half full battery charges, but only getting one. For new EV owners transitioning from petrol cars, this is a completely foreign concept. You would never leave your petrol nozzle in the tank and go shopping, and you must treat EV lots with the exact same urgency.

## How to Protect Yourself

- **Enable Push Notifications:** Operators like SP Mobility send multiple alerts—when charging completes, halfway through the grace period, and right before fees start. You will be billed regardless of whether you saw the notification.
- **Know Your Battery Size:** Do the math for your specific vehicle. If you drive a car with a smaller battery, a $40 cap is a massive financial penalty compared to your usual charging costs.
